01 — What's Changing

This Is Not a Policy Update. This Is a New Regime.

AMLR does not amend the existing framework — it replaces it. Institutions that approach it as a document revision exercise will discover at examination that supervisors are not assessing whether a document exists. They are assessing whether there is an evidenced, operational, tested programme behind it.

01
Direct Applicability — No Local Buffer
WWFT was a Dutch implementation with local interpretation. AMLR applies directly across 27 Member States. Same obligations. Same examination standard. Same enforcement ceiling. National flexibility and interpretation margins are gone.
02
AMLA — Already Collecting Your Data
The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority is not a future concept — it is operational and already executing. As of March 2026, AMLA has launched a data collection exercise across approximately 5,000 sampled institutions, with a submission deadline of 22 April 2026. This data feeds directly into AMLA's risk assessment and selection methodology for determining which institutions it will supervise directly from 2027. Credit institutions, payment institutions, investment firms, and asset management companies managing UCITS or AIFs are all within scope. National supervisors face pressure to demonstrate rigour. That pressure is already flowing to you.
03
Expanded & Sharper Obligations
CDD threshold lowered to €10,000. Harmonised UBO at 25%. Mandatory five-day SAR response window. Enhanced compliance officer requirements. New outsourcing rules. Crypto-asset providers and crowdfunding platforms now obliged entities.
04
Enforcement Ceiling: €10M or 10% Turnover
AMLD6 raises maximum pecuniary sanctions to €10 million or 10% of total annual turnover for serious, repeated, or systematic breaches. Combined with the Dutch track record, the financial exposure of incomplete implementation has never been higher.

The Central Question

Not "do we have a policy?" — but "can we demonstrate, to a supervisor we may not have dealt with before, against a standard we have not yet been examined against, that our AML/CFT framework is operational, tested, and embedded across all three lines of defence?" The distance between these two questions is the distance between compliance and enforcement.

02 — Why It Matters

The Dutch Market Has Already Answered This Question

The enforcement record is not instructive because these institutions were careless. It is instructive because the pattern is identical in every case. Compliance identified the gaps. Audit confirmed them. Management did not convert them into a governed programme.

Proactive

The Uncomfortable Arithmetic

A structured AMLR implementation programme costs a fraction of post-enforcement remediation. But the difference is not only financial. It is the difference between presenting your framework to a supervisor on your terms — with confidence, with evidence, with a programme owner who can walk them through it — versus having it examined under enforcement conditions where the dynamic is fundamentally adversarial.

Hard Deadline

What the AMLA Data Collection Means in Practice

AMLA's 2026 exercise covers every category of obliged entity eligible for direct supervision — credit institutions, payment institutions, e-money institutions, investment firms, and asset management companies managing UCITS or AIFs. The reporting template requires granular data on customer composition, PEP-connected beneficial owners, legal entities with complex structures, cross-border geographic exposure, and the quality of AML/CFT governance and controls — reported at solo entity level, not consolidated group level. For banks with extensive correspondent networks, PSPs processing high-volume cross-border transactions, or asset managers with collective investment vehicles spanning multiple jurisdictions, the data extraction challenge alone tests whether the AML/CFT framework can produce what AMLA is asking for. Institutions that struggle with the data collection are demonstrating — in real time, to their national supervisor — exactly the kind of framework weakness that elevates the risk profile AMLA uses for its 2027 selection decisions.

04 — The Examination Map

12 Domains Supervisors Will Assess

Each domain below is a supervisory examination area under AMLR. Supervisors assess whether these connect into a coherent, operational whole — not whether individual documents exist. Based on Dutch enforcement patterns and supervisory practice, 7 domains consistently present the highest remediation burden when gaps are found.

01
Governance & Target Operating Model
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
Named AMLR programme owner with dedicated mandate — not shared. Documented TOM showing how AML/CFT operates across all three lines. RACI for every process. Most institutions have policies. Far fewer have a TOM. The difference is what separates a document collection from a framework.
02
SIRA / BWRA & Risk Appetite
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
BWRA with documented scoring methodology and weighting rationale. Risk Appetite Statement quantifying tolerance. Customer Risk Assessment designed for your profiles — not inherited. AMLA's 2026 data collection exercise requires institutions to report granular risk datapoints — customer composition, PEP exposure, complex structure prevalence, geographic distribution — that directly test whether the institution's own risk assessment methodology can produce consistent, auditable numbers. Supervisors ask: "Show me how you arrived at this score." If your BWRA cannot reproduce the data AMLA is now requesting, the methodology gap is already visible.
03
KYC Policy & Procedure Suite
Significant
Expand ↓
L1/L2/L3 transposed to AMLR. Procedures reflecting what staff actually do, not what was documented three years ago. Deviation processes with rationale. Four-eyes evidenced in workflow. Policy updated but procedure unchanged is the gap profile supervisors identify most frequently.
04
CDD / EDD Framework
Significant
Expand ↓
AMLR materially changes CDD triggers, EDD requirements, and UBO verification. For institutions with cross-border exposure, multi-layered fund structures, pooled investment vehicles, or institutional mandates where the ultimate beneficial owner sits several layers removed from the direct counterparty, the gap between current practice and AMLR requirements is typically substantial. The most common examination finding: "Your CDD is adequate for direct, transparent counterparties and inadequate for the complex, multi-jurisdictional structures that constitute the majority of your portfolio."
05
Transaction Monitoring
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
Consistently the domain where the largest gap exists between belief and evidence. Documented scenario library. Threshold calibration with rationale. Scenario injection testing. Independent model validation. Most institutions have TM. Very few can demonstrate it is calibrated for their profiles rather than vendor defaults. The question is not whether your TM works. It is whether you can prove it.
06
UTR / FIU-NL Reporting
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
Quality, consistency, and rationale behind filing decisions. Defensive filing attracts criticism. Not filing when indicators were present attracts enforcement. Institutions formalising TM for the first time face legacy exposure from historical non-filing periods — a material risk most do not assess until it is too late.
07
PEP & Adverse Media
Significant
Expand ↓
Tiered PEP risk assessment. Adverse media with source hierarchy, language coverage, and relevance criteria. Proportionate ongoing monitoring at documented frequency and depth. Supervisors ask: "What triggers enhanced monitoring? How often? Who decides? Where is it documented?"
08
Sanctions & Screening
Significant
Expand ↓
AMLR is not a sanctions regulation per se — the EU sanctions framework operates under separate legal instruments (Council Regulations under CFSP). However, AMLR embeds screening obligations directly into the CDD and ongoing monitoring architecture, requiring institutions to demonstrate that sanctions screening is integrated into their AML/CFT framework rather than operating as a standalone function. For institutions managing pooled investment vehicles, multi-jurisdictional fund structures, or portfolios with underlying investors across multiple regulatory perimeters, the screening challenge is structurally different from retail banking — beneficial ownership chains through nominee structures, sub-funds, and institutional mandates create screening complexity that standard tools routinely miss. EU, UN, OFAC, UK list screening. Retroactive screening on list updates. Fuzzy logic calibration with documented match-score thresholds and validated methodology. The current geopolitical environment has elevated sanctions screening from a periodic compliance check to a priority examination area — supervisors now expect documented evidence of how screening is calibrated for the institution's specific counterparty and investor profile.
09
Training & Competency
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
Competency testing — understanding, not attendance. Role-specific for institutional complexity. Board awareness of personal liability. An institution with excellent policies and untrained staff presents the same risk profile as one with no policies at all.
10
Exit & De-Risking
Significant
Expand ↓
Exit procedure with defined triggers and governance approval. UTR assessment at exit. Anti-blanket de-risking obligations. Supervisors now sanction disproportionate de-risking — the compliance challenge runs both ways.
11
QA & Effectiveness Testing
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
Most institutions test individual controls. Supervisors assess whether the framework as a whole is effective. Statistical sampling. Analyst benchmarking. Root-cause analysis. If you cannot produce this evidence today, the question is whether the timeline to build it fits within the window remaining.
12
Regulatory Watch & RTS Integration
High Enforcement Risk
Expand ↓
80+ binding standards in consultation through 2027. Impact assessment per publication. Documented response decisions. Board reporting on the regulatory pipeline. Without a structured tracking function, institutions discover the cumulative effect of these standards too late to respond proportionately.

07 — Self-Assessment

Six Questions That Determine Your Exposure

These are derived from actual supervisory examination frameworks. The confidence or hesitation with which your institution can answer them is itself a diagnostic.

01
Programme Ownership
Who owns your AMLR implementation — and do they have a dedicated mandate, or is it one of several responsibilities?
Distributed ownership is the most consistent root cause in Dutch AML enforcement. AMLR requires a programme — and a programme requires an owner.
02
Framework Evidence
If your supervisor requested your Target Operating Model today — could you produce one? Would it reflect how your institution actually operates?
A TOM produced for a previous review and not maintained is worse than no TOM at all — it demonstrates awareness of the requirement and failure to sustain it.
03
TM Calibration
Can you demonstrate that your transaction monitoring is calibrated for your actual transaction profiles — with documented rationale for every threshold?
The gap between "we have TM" and "we can evidence TM effectiveness" is where the largest remediation items consistently land.
04
Staff Competency
If a supervisor interviewed your CDD analysts about AMLR's specific requirements — would they pass?
Policy without operational understanding is a document, not a control. Supervisors increasingly test this directly. Attendance records do not answer the question.
05
Regulatory Tracking & Data Readiness
Is someone monitoring each AMLA/EBA publication, assessing relevance, and documenting the response decision? And could your institution produce the structured AML/CFT risk data AMLA is now requesting from sampled entities?
80+ standards expected, plus active data collection exercises. Institutions not tracking these are building against an incomplete specification — and those that cannot produce their own risk data on demand are signalling the gap AMLA is designed to find.
06
The Real Question
If AFM or DNB requested a readiness review next month — not in July 2027, but next month — what would you present?
Supervisors do not always announce examinations in advance. The answer is the single most accurate measure of your actual exposure.

How to Read Your Answers

If all six answers came with documented evidence and immediate confidence — your institution is in a minority. If two or more prompted hesitation, the need to check with a colleague, or the quiet recognition that the honest answer is "I'm not sure" — that is your supervisory exposure. Most Dutch financial institutions completed their AMLR gap assessment in summer 2025 and are now in active remediation. The institutions that paid €775 million and €480 million could have answered these questions too. They answered them the same way.
